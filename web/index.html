How the map is made and validated
  1. Sample points. A regular grid of 49–121 points covers the area (7 × 7 up to 5 km, 9 × 9 up to 10 km, 11 × 11 above). At each point the value is computed from the Open-Meteo atmosphere (ECMWF IFS, CAMS aerosols) with the 6S tables (or, for the forecast, with the GPR). These are the “known” values: click a validation point to see it.
  2. Trend. A linear regression of these values on Copernicus covariates at 10 m: elevation, slope and northness (Copernicus DEM / EU-DEM), imperviousness and tree-cover density (EEA High Resolution Layers 2018). Covariates that are constant over the area are left out.
  3. Kriging of the residuals. What the trend does not explain is interpolated with ordinary kriging (exponential variogram fitted on the residuals). Map = trend + kriged residual; the kriging standard deviation is its uncertainty.
  4. Validation (leave-one-out). Each sample point is removed in turn, the whole model is fitted again on the others and the value at the removed point is predicted. The error table compares RK with the trend alone and with kriging alone. On the map, the size of each validation point is its error. This checks the interpolation between the points, not the accuracy of the atmospheric data themselves.

Imperviousness is the share of the ground sealed by buildings, roads and other artificial surfaces (0–100 % per pixel, EEA HRL 2018). It marks urban areas, which are warmer (urban heat island) and can have more aerosols near the ground.

How to read the GPR tables

Does the GPR really beat the individual providers? It is checked in two independent ways, always on data never used for training:

  1. Historical back-test (weekly): the record is split into 14-day test blocks; for each block the GPR is trained only on the previous 45 days, and the “Best” provider and the weights are also chosen only from those days, exactly as it happens in real time. The table shows the error over all blocks and how often the GPR beat the Best provider.
  2. Latest days (daily): the same test on the most recent 14 days.

If the uncertainty is correct, the observed value falls inside the 68% interval about 68 times out of 100 and inside the 95% interval about 95 times out of 100. Green cells: well-calibrated uncertainty; amber/red: use with care. “Best ex post” is the model that, in hindsight, did best on those days: a reference no method can know in advance.
