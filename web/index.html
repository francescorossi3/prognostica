How the terms are related: radiance at the sensor

VNIR–SWIR (0.4–2.5 µm), Lambertian surface

ρsensor = Tgas · [ ρpath + T↓ · T↑ · ρ / (1 − S · ρ) ]
Lsensor = E0 · cos θs · ρsensor / π

with Lpath ≈ E0 cos θs ρpath / π and Edirect + Ediffuse ≈ E0 cos θs T↓ (6S formulation).

ρ
Surface reflectance: what atmospheric correction wants to recover.
T↓ scattering
Downward transmittance (Sun → ground) left by scattering of molecules (Rayleigh) and aerosols, direct + diffuse.
T↑ scattering
Upward transmittance (ground → sensor) left by scattering, direct + diffuse.
T gas
Transmittance of gas absorption (water vapour, ozone, O₂, CO₂, CH₄…) along the Sun – ground – sensor path.
Total T (↓·↑·gas)
Share of the surface signal that reaches the sensor (without the multiple reflections).
Path radiance [W m⁻² sr⁻¹ µm⁻¹]
Light scattered by the atmosphere into the sensor without touching the ground: a veil that is added to the signal, strongest in the blue.
E direct [W m⁻² µm⁻¹]
Solar irradiance reaching a horizontal ground directly from the Sun.
E diffuse [W m⁻² µm⁻¹]
Irradiance reaching the ground from the whole sky, after scattering.
Spherical albedo S
Reflectance of the atmosphere for light coming from below: the term 1/(1 − S·ρ) counts the reflections back and forth between ground and atmosphere.
E0, θs
Solar irradiance at the top of the atmosphere and solar zenith angle.

LWIR (8–14 µm)

Lsensor = τ · [ ε · B(Ts) + (1 − ε) · L↓ ] + L↑

τ
Atmospheric transmittance between ground and sensor (mostly water vapour).
L↑
Upwelling path radiance emitted by the atmosphere towards the sensor.
L↓
Downwelling radiance emitted by the atmosphere towards the ground (hemispheric, divided by π), partly reflected by the surface.
ε
Surface emissivity.
B(Ts)
Planck radiance of a black body at the land surface temperature Ts.

6S does not cover the LWIR: τ, L↑ and L↓ come from libRadtran.

Validation against measurements (AERONET)

Water vapour (ECMWF IFS, about 25 km) and aerosol optical depth (CAMS, about 45 km) corrected with covariates at the time of each Sentinel-2 image (Sentinel-2 water vapour and AOT, reflectances and indices at 60, 500 and 2000 m, terrain) by random forest or gradient boosting, with kriging of the residuals. Each site is tested while left out of the training: the errors are against real sun-photometer measurements.

How the map is made
  1. Sample points. A regular grid of 49–121 points covers the area (7 × 7 up to 5 km, 9 × 9 up to 10 km, 11 × 11 above). At each point the value is computed from the Open-Meteo atmosphere (ECMWF IFS, CAMS aerosols) with the 6S tables (or, for the forecast, with the GPR).
  2. Trend. A linear regression of these values on the ground elevation at 10 m (Copernicus DEM / EU-DEM). Elevation is the only 10 m quantity that changes the atmosphere above the ground (less air and water vapour above high ground); land-cover layers are not used because they are not contemporaneous with the atmosphere.
  3. Kriging of the residuals. What the trend does not explain is interpolated with ordinary kriging (exponential variogram fitted on the residuals). Map = trend + kriged residual; the kriging standard deviation is its uncertainty.

Input data. Water vapour column, pressure and temperature come from ECMWF IFS at 0.25° (about 25 km); the aerosol optical depth at 550 nm from CAMS global at 0.4° (about 45 km). Open-Meteo returns the value of the grid cell of each point, so nearby sample points often share the same values. At each point the 6S look-up table is interpolated in water vapour, AOD, solar zenith angle and the elevation of the point.

This map interpolates model values and is not checked against measurements; the validated correction with Sentinel-2 is shown above.

How to read the GPR tables

Does the GPR really beat the individual providers? It is checked in two independent ways, always on data never used for training:

  1. Historical back-test (weekly): the record is split into 14-day test blocks; for each block the GPR is trained only on the previous 45 days, and the “Best” provider and the weights are also chosen only from those days, exactly as it happens in real time. The table shows the error over all blocks and how often the GPR beat the Best provider.
  2. Latest days (daily): the same test on the most recent 14 days.

If the uncertainty is correct, the observed value falls inside the 68% interval about 68 times out of 100 and inside the 95% interval about 95 times out of 100. Green cells: well-calibrated uncertainty; amber/red: use with care. “Best ex post” is the model that, in hindsight, did best on those days: a reference no method can know in advance.
